# What OBDb's own data says per model year

Generated by `research/tools/evidence-obdb.js` from the OBDb vehicle repositories (CC BY-SA 4.0): the signal sets' `filter` and `dbgfilter`,
the year-range signal sets, and the replies recorded under `tests/test_cases/<model year>/`. It covers the signals the Service and Mileage pages
classify as odometer-like or service-like. **Nothing here was measured on a vehicle by OBD360.** A recorded reply is a reply somebody else recorded and
OBDb's CI checks against the definition; it shows that a car of that model year answered and what the definition makes of the answer.

## Why the earlier counts overstate support

The shipped catalog keeps one flag per row, the command-level `dbg`. OBDb also has `dbgfilter` (debug in some model years only) and `filter` (the years a
command applies to). Both were dropped on import: **61,598 catalog rows carry a `dbgfilter` upstream and are marked "not debug"**, and no row can be
restricted to a model year. So "not flagged debug" never meant "verified for this car". Evidence tiers used below (per signal, over all model years):

* **A**: a reply was recorded in OBDb's tests for at least one model year.
* **B**: applicable and not debug in at least one model year, but no recorded reply.
* **C**: debug in every applicable year.

The Mileage page's "requestable" figure (457 vehicles with a current-odometer definition) is a count of definitions. The same signals by evidence:

* vehicles with a recorded reply for at least one current-odometer definition: **334**;
* vehicles with two modules that both have recorded odometer values in the same model year: **140**;
* of those, vehicles where the values of two modules track each other: **116** (423 of 695 module pairs in a model year).

"Track each other" is a heuristic: distances are converted to kilometres with the unit each definition declares, and a pair tracks when at least three values
agree within max(50 km, 0.2 %) and at least half of the smaller list does. Recorded replies of different commands can come from different cars, so this shows that two
definitions read the same kind of number, not that two modules of one car agree. A pair that does not track (47 pairs) is not proof of a wrong definition either.

## Definitions that hold something else than their name says

* **BMW-3-Series** `3SERIES_OIL_CHANGE_DIST` "Distance since last oil change", `22 DFE7` @ 6F1/19: 49 of 72 recorded values (model years 2019-2022) lie within 50 km (or 0.2 %) of an odometer value recorded for the same model year; recorded range 17724 to 100022 km.
* **BMW-5-Series** `5SERIES_OIL_CHANGE_DIST` "Distance since last oil change", `22 DFE7` @ 6F1/19: 74 of 134 recorded values (model years 2018-2022) lie within 50 km (or 0.2 %) of an odometer value recorded for the same model year; recorded range 19664 to 239253 km.
* **BMW-7-Series** `7SERIES_OIL_CHANGE_DIST` "Distance since last oil change", `22 DFE7` @ 6F1/19: 30 of 52 recorded values (model years 2017, 2020) lie within 50 km (or 0.2 %) of an odometer value recorded for the same model year; recorded range 5989 to 158000 km.
* **BMW-X5** `X5_OIL_CHANGE_DIST` "Distance since last oil change", `22 DFE7` @ 6F1/19: 51 of 83 recorded values (model years 2020-2022) lie within 50 km (or 0.2 %) of an odometer value recorded for the same model year; recorded range 2268 to 166000 km.

The BMW definition is shared by 19 BMW models, 4 MINI models and the Toyota GR Supra (`22 DFE7` at module 0x19, bit 304); the app now never shows it as an oil-change distance.

### Recorded values that are not a distance

44 current-odometer definitions have a recorded reply that decodes to more than 2,000,000 km (all-ones fields such as 4,294,967,295, 2,147,483,647 or 16,777,215, or a scale that does not fit). By make: Volkswagen 15, Audi 14, Seat 6, Lexus 2, Renault 2, BMW 1, Cupra 1, Ford 1, GMC 1, Toyota 1. The Mileage page already treats such a value as implausible and does not use it.

### Units

5 module pairs match only after one more mile/kilometre factor, which means one of the two definitions declares the wrong unit: Lexus-NX 700 vs 7D2 (2024); Subaru-Impreza 7A2 vs 7E0 (2024); Toyota-bZ4X 747 vs 7D2 (2023); Toyota-Tundra 700 vs 7D2 (2023); Volkswagen-Passat 714 vs 7E1 (2019). They need a vehicle to say which one.

## Limits

* Only the signals the app classifies (by name) are covered. The model year is the folder name of the test; OBDb does not say how the contributors determined it.
* A recorded reply says nothing about the ECU on a car that was not tested, and a missing one does not say the definition is wrong.
* The year-range signal sets (`YYYY-YYYY.json`) exist in very few repositories; where they do, the one containing the year replaces `default.json`, as OBDb's loader does.
* The module of a signal is the header (and extended address) of its command; OBDb states a module type for only some headers.
